MORE THAN ONE KIND OF STUCK

Which part feels difficult?

Open only what is useful. No choice here commits you to a change.

A job

Compare income during a gap, commuting, health coverage, notice requirements, and a possible start date. Is a change of hours, role, or employer worth exploring? The calculator can compare either option.

Caregiving

What support is available for the person receiving care—and for you? Questions might include respite care, paid help, transport, shared responsibilities, and costs. A local service can help you understand available support without deciding for you.

Living with family

Compare contributions at home with rent, deposits, utilities, food, and transport elsewhere. Consider shared housing or a gradual change if those fit your circumstances. Your pace does not need to match anyone else’s.

A city or country

Compare housing and moving costs, access to work, language, healthcare, and support nearby. For an international move, verify permission to live and work with the destination’s official services; immigration rules and benefits vary.

A relationship or household

Money is one part of a decision. Control, safety, children, housing, and access to support also matter. You can speak with an advocate without committing to leave. If someone monitors your devices, consider a safer device before exploring sensitive options.

What the help actually does

These explanations concern U.S. services. Eligibility, availability, and privacy practices differ. Links open external sites in this tab.

Legal aid

Legal aid organizations offer free civil legal help to eligible people, often those with low incomes. Cases may involve housing, family matters, or benefits. Income, location, the issue, and available staff affect eligibility; ask the local provider rather than assuming you qualify or do not.

Find local legal aid ↗

Child support estimates

Use your state or tribe’s official agency or court to find its calculator, if available. It may ask about income, parenting time, childcare, and insurance. An estimate is not an order or a payment date. Do not treat an estimated award as cash already available.

Find the responsible agency ↗

211: local options

211 can connect people with local housing, food, utility assistance, benefits, and other services. It is a referral service, not a promise of funding or an available bed. Ask what information is required and how it will be used.

Explore 211 housing help ↗

Your credit reports

Reports can help you spot accounts or debts you do not recognize. The official free-report route is AnnualCreditReport.com. A report is not a complete list of every debt. If something looks wrong, the CFPB explains how to review and dispute it.

CFPB: get your reports ↗

An account of your own

A bank or credit union will verify identity; accepted documents, deposits, and fees vary. Ask about minimum balances, overdraft charges, and when deposits become available. If privacy is a concern, consider where mail, verification codes, and alerts could appear. This is not advice to transfer jointly owned money.

What may come next

A planning sequence, not a countdown. These stages may overlap, take months, happen in another order, or never be needed. Staying, pausing, and revisiting are valid options. Urgent safety support does not depend on completing a budget.

WHEN EXPLORING

Understand the unknowns

People often compare housing, ongoing bills, access to money, and support. A confidential conversation with a qualified service may clarify what matters before any change.

BEFORE A POSSIBLE CHANGE

Confirm practical details

A place to stay, transport, care arrangements, and access to essentials may need checking. Ask relevant providers when coverage or payments stop or start. An advocate can help with safety; legal aid can explain which filings, if any, apply.

DURING A TRANSITION

Check timing, not just totals

Deposits and fees may be due before income arrives. Confirm due dates and arrangements directly. Filing a claim or application does not guarantee approval, payment, or a particular date.

AS THINGS SETTLE

Revisit the estimates

Actual bills may differ. People may need to review care, housing, coverage, and any legal deadlines with the appropriate service. There is no required pace or single correct outcome.

IMMEDIATE DANGER

Emergency assistance

If you or someone else is in immediate danger in the U.S., call 911 if it is safe to do so. If possible, seek a safer location and contact a qualified local service.

OUTSIDE THE UNITED STATES

Find local support

Lila.help, from the Global Network of Women’s Shelters, lists gender-based violence services in many countries. Languages, hours, and who each service supports vary; check the listing. In immediate danger, use your local emergency service if safe.
